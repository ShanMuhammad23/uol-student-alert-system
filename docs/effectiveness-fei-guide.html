Faculty & Department Effectiveness Guide

Student Early Alert System — FEI metrics, scoring formulas, and plain-language explanations
Document version: June 2026 · Matches implementation in src/lib/effectiveness.ts

Purpose: Measure whether faculties and departments are using the alert system to support student wellbeing — not just to generate alerts.

1. What is FEI?

FEI (Faculty Effectiveness Index) is a score from 0 to 100 with a letter grade A–E. It combines how quickly and thoroughly staff respond to at-risk students, whether students are referred to wellbeing, whether students recover academically, and whether attendance data is reliable.

In simple terms: FEI answers: “When the system flags a struggling student, does this faculty actually help them — and do students get better?”
GradeFEI rangeMeaning
A85 – 100Exemplary — strong outreach, wellbeing pathway, and recovery
B70 – 84Effective — good practice with minor gaps
C55 – 69Developing — alerts exist but response or outcomes are weak
D40 – 54At risk — significant gaps in student support
E< 40Critical — intervention and wellbeing support largely absent

2. Overall FEI formula

FEI = round( 0.30 × Outcome + 0.25 × Wellbeing + 0.25 × Response + 0.10 × Readiness + 0.10 × Sustained , 2 )
ComponentWeightWhat it measures
Outcome30%Students improving after intervention; few repeat alerts
Wellbeing25%Referrals to wellbeing and confirmed wellbeing cases
Response25%Coverage of alerted students, speed, open cases
Readiness10%Attendance posting quality (data trust)
Sustained10%Same as outcome focus: recovery + repeat control

3. Percentage helper (used everywhere)

For any rate with numerator n and denominator d:

If d > 0:   Rate% = round( (n ÷ d) × 100 , 2 decimal places )
If d = 0:   Rate% = empty (shown as “—” on dashboard)

5. Dashboard columns — formula & plain explanation

5.1 Coverage (Intervention coverage %)

Coverage% = pct( Nintervened , Nalerted )
Plain English: Of all students the system flagged as at-risk, what percentage did staff actually reach out to?
Example: 165 alerted, 74 intervened → 74÷165 = 44.9%

5.2 Critical coverage %

Critical coverage% = pct( Ncrit intervened , Ncrit alerted )
Plain English: Same as coverage, but only for the most serious (critical) cases. Critical students should be prioritised.

5.3 TTFC — Time to first contact (median days)

For each student S:
ΔS = (date of first intervention − date of first alert) in days
TTFC = median of all ΔS where both dates exist
Plain English: How many days pass, on average, between the system first spotting a problem and a staff member making contact? Lower is better. Shown as “—” if daily alert history or interventions are missing.

5.4 Wellbeing uptake %

Wellbeing% = pct( Nwellbeing , Nreferred )
Plain English: When staff refer a student toward wellbeing support, how often does that student actually appear in the wellbeing centre’s case records? This checks the handoff is real, not just a label on a form.

5.5 Recovery (Alert recovery %)

Recovery% = pct( Nrecovered , Nintervened )
Plain English: After staff intervened, how many students cleared their alert (attendance/GPA no longer in warning/critical)? This is the closest measure of “did support work?”

5.6 Alerted (count)

Alerted = Nalerted
Plain English: How many students are currently flagged as needing attention in this faculty. This is workload / risk volume, not a quality score by itself.

5.7 Hidden detail metrics (expand row on dashboard)

MetricFormulaPlain English
Referral rate % pct( Nreferred , Nintervened ) Of students staff contacted, how many were referred onward (e.g. wellbeing)?
Repeat alert % pct( Nrepeat , Nalerted ) Students back in alert after a case was already closed — may mean shallow follow-up
Stale intervention % pct( Nstale , Nopen ) Open cases left unattended for more than 14 days
Attendance posting % 100 × Σ marked classes ÷ Σ classes held How completely instructors post attendance — affects alert accuracy

6. How each parameter becomes a score (0–100)

Every percentage or day-value is converted to a sub-score from 0 to 100 using fixed bands. If data is missing (“—”), the sub-score defaults to 50 (neutral).

7. Component scores (combined from parameters)

7.1 Response score (25% of FEI)

Response = average of:
  f↑( Coverage% ; 95, 85, 70, 50 )
  f↑( Critical coverage% ; 95, 90, 75, 60 )
  f↓( TTFC days ; 3, 7, 14, 21 )
  f↓( Stale% ; 10, 20, 35, 50 )
Plain English: Are we reaching flagged students quickly, including critical cases, and not leaving cases open too long?

7.2 Wellbeing score (25% of FEI)

Wellbeing = average of:
  f↑( Referral rate% ; 40, 25, 15, 8 )
  f↑( Wellbeing uptake% ; 80, 65, 50, 35 )
Plain English: When academic staff identify distress, are students referred appropriately and does the wellbeing service receive them?

7.3 Outcome score (30% of FEI)

Outcome = average of:
  f↑( Recovery% ; 60, 45, 30, 15 )
  f↓( Repeat alert% ; 5, 10, 20, 30 )
Plain English: Do students actually improve after support, and do we avoid students slipping back into crisis after a closed case?

7.4 Readiness score (10% of FEI)

Readiness = f↑( Attendance posting% ; 95, 90, 80, 70 )
Plain English: Is attendance data complete enough that alerts can be trusted? Poor posting can hide or distort student risk.

7.5 Sustained score (10% of FEI)

Sustained = average of:
  f↑( Recovery% ; 60, 45, 30, 15 )
  f↓( Repeat alert% ; 5, 10, 20, 30 )
Plain English: Same ideas as Outcome — recovery and avoiding repeat crises — weighted a second time to emphasise long-term student wellbeing.

10. Important notes for readers

  • FEI is not a punishment tool — it highlights where student support processes need strengthening.
  • High “Alerted” does not mean failure — it often means the detection system is working.
  • Low coverage with high alerts means risk is visible but outreach has not kept pace.
  • Scores update daily after the ETL job runs (/api/cron/effectiveness).
  • “Live compute” on the dashboard recalculates from current data without waiting for the nightly snapshot.
