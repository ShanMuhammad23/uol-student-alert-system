Student Early Alert System

Staff Training Guide — Alerts, Access & Interventions

University of Lahore

For instructors, HoDs, deans, and wellbeing staff · UI walkthrough only

1. Welcome — what this system does

The Student Early Alert System helps the university spot students whose attendance or SGPA (semester GPA) is falling, so staff can reach out early — before problems become crises.

Your role is not only to see alerts, but to:

  1. Contact the student and understand what is going wrong
  2. Record what you did (an “intervention”) in the system
  3. Follow up until the case is resolved or the student is referred to the Wellbeing Centre
Think of the system as: Detect → Discuss → Document → Support → Resolve.

2. Signing in

  1. Open the system in your web browser (link provided by your IT/admin team).
  2. Go to the Sign in page.
  3. Enter your university email and password.
  4. After login, you are taken automatically to the screen that matches your role (see Section 3).

If you cannot sign in, contact your faculty administrator — do not share your password.

3. Who sees what — your access level

Each person sees only the students and data relevant to their job.

RoleWhat you see after login
Instructor / TeacherDashboard for your own courses and students. You add interventions for students you teach.
Head of Department (HoD)Dashboard for your department(s) — all programs, courses, and instructors under you.
DeanDashboard for your whole faculty — departments, programs, courses, instructors. Can also open Faculty staff (view-only directory).
Wellbeing counsellorWellbeing Dashboard — students referred to you; add resolutions and direct cases.
Wellbeing headWellbeing Admin — overview of all wellbeing cases.
System administratorFull university view, staff management, and automation screens (not covered in this guide).
Remember: You only see students in your scope. If you search for a student and find nothing, they may belong to another department or faculty.

4. Types of alerts — what the colours mean

The system watches attendance and SGPA for every enrolled student. When something is wrong, the student gets an alert. On screen you will see Yellow / Warning or Red / Critical.

4.1 Attendance alerts

ColourNameWhat it means (in plain language)
Red Critical Student attendance is 60% or below (and classes have been held).
Yellow Warning Attendance is above 60%, but the student is at least 20 percentage points below the class average (e.g. class average 85%, student 60%).

4.2 SGPA (grade) alerts

ColourNameWhat it means
Red Critical SGPA has dropped by 1.5 points or more compared to the previous semester.
Yellow Warning SGPA has dropped by 1.0 to 1.49 points compared to the previous semester.

4.3 Combined alerts

A student can have an attendance alert, an SGPA alert, or both. If either is red, treat the case as high priority.

Important: An alert is a signal to talk to the student, not a punishment. Many students are struggling with health, family, or financial issues — your conversation matters.

5. The main dashboard — what you see

After login, most academic staff land on the Dashboard. It has four main areas:

5.1 Overview cards (top row)

  • Total students — how many students are in your scope.
  • Attendance alerts — yellow and red counts. Click a card to filter the student list below.
  • SGPA alerts — yellow and red counts (may be hidden for some roles until grade data is available).

5.2 Outreach & Intervention chart

Shows how alert cases are moving through the support pipeline:

Status shownMeaning
Not StartedStudent is in alert, but no intervention has been recorded yet.
InitiatedFirst contact recorded — outreach has begun.
In ProgressFollow-up is ongoing (second or later contact on the same course).
ReferredStudent referred to the Wellbeing Centre (or similar support).
ResolvedIssue addressed — student improved or situation stabilised.
No Action RequiredReviewed and closed — no further action needed (use with care and brief notes).

5.3 Wellbeing chart

Shows referred students and wellbeing case progress (mainly for deans/HoDs monitoring handoffs).

5.4 Student list (main table)

A large table of students, courses, and alert levels. Use the filters at the top to narrow by department, program, instructor, course, alert type, or intervention status.

Tip: Click column headers or filter chips to find “red attendance, not started” students first — they need the fastest response.

6. How to find a student

There are three common ways:

Method A — Browse the dashboard table

  1. On the Dashboard, scroll to the student table.
  2. Use filters (department, course, alert colour) to shorten the list.
  3. Click the student name or row to open their profile.

Method B — Intervention search tab

  1. On the Dashboard, switch to the Intervention search view (tab at top of the student area).
  2. Enter the student’s SAP ID (student number).
  3. Press Search — you see all their courses, alert types, and latest intervention status.
  4. Open the student profile from there.

Method C — From a specific course

  1. Filter the dashboard to one course or expand a nested course view.
  2. Find the student in that class list and open their profile.

7. Student profile page — what you will see

When you open a student, you see:

  • Student details — name, SAP ID, faculty, department, program.
  • Course metrics — attendance %, class average, SGPA/CGPA, alert badges per course.
  • Intervention History — all past interventions and emails sent.

If you arrived from a specific course row, the page may focus on that course and section — interventions you add will be linked to that class.

8. How to add an intervention — step by step

1 Open the student’s profile (Section 6).
2 Scroll to Intervention History.
3 Click the green Add Intervention button.
4 A form opens. Fill in each field (explained below).
5 Click Add Intervention at the bottom of the form.
6 You should see a success message. The new row appears in Intervention History.

8.1 Form fields explained

FieldWhat to enter
Date The date you contacted or met the student (usually today).
Type Attendance — mainly discussing attendance.
SGPA — mainly discussing grades.
Both — both issues discussed.
Instructors may only see Attendance if SGPA is restricted for their role.
Status → No Action Required Tick only if you reviewed the alert and genuinely no follow-up is needed. Hides the outreach mode field. Always add a short remark explaining why.
Mode How you reached the student: Email, WhatsApp, Phone Call, Meeting, or Not Applicable.
Concluding Status Use only when closing or escalating:
Referred — sending student to Wellbeing Centre.
Resolved — case closed; student is OK or back on track.
If you leave this blank, the system sets Initiated or In Progress automatically.
Remarks Short notes: what the student said, agreed actions, concerns (stress, financial, health). Do not put sensitive medical detail — refer to Wellbeing instead.

8.2 The progress stepper on the form

At the top of the form you may see three steps: Initiated → In Progress → Concluding Status. This shows where the case sits in the journey. Your first contact usually starts at Initiated.

8.3 Automatic status rules (you do not need to memorise these)

  • First intervention on a course → usually saved as Initiated.
  • Another intervention on the same course when earlier ones were only “Initiated” → moves to In Progress.
  • If you select Referred or Resolved → saved exactly as chosen.

9. Sending an email from the intervention form

If you choose Mode = Email, an email section appears below the form.

9.1 SOS Check-In email

Use for a first friendly check-in with the student about attendance or SGPA. The system fills in the student’s name, SAP ID, and academic details. Review the text, then send.

When: First outreach, status is Initiated, mode is Email.

9.2 Student Referral email

Use when you select Referred as concluding status — to notify the Wellbeing Centre or counsellor that the student needs support.

When: You have decided the student needs professional wellbeing support.

Always use your official university email as the reply address. The system logs every email sent for audit purposes.

10. Following up after you add an intervention

Adding an intervention is not the end. You must track the case until it is truly finished.

10.1 What to do next — general timeline

WhenYour action
Within 3–7 days of first contactFollow up if the student has not responded or situation unchanged.
After each contactAdd another intervention OR update the existing one’s status.
If student mentions stress, depression, family crisisChange status to Referred and involve Wellbeing.
When attendance/SGPA improvesMark as Resolved with a note on what improved.

10.2 How to update an existing intervention

  1. Go to the student’s profile → Intervention History.
  2. Find the intervention row you want to update.
  3. Click Edit (pencil icon or Edit button on that row).
  4. Change Status, Mode, or Date as needed.
  5. Click Save Changes.

Available statuses when editing:

  • Initiated
  • In Progress
  • Referred
  • Resolved
  • No Action Required

10.3 Adding a follow-up intervention (instead of editing)

Sometimes it is better to add a new intervention for each contact (second meeting, second call) so there is a clear history. Click Add Intervention again and record the new date and remarks.

10.4 Monitoring on the dashboard

Return to your Dashboard and check:

  • The Outreach & Intervention chart — “Not Started” should go down as you work cases.
  • Filter the student table by intervention status to see who is still In Progress or Referred.

11. Referring a student to Wellbeing

  1. Talk to the student first where possible — explain that support is available.
  2. On the intervention form, set Concluding Status → Referred.
  3. In Remarks, note the reason (academic stress, mental health concern, financial difficulty) without excessive private detail.
  4. If using email, send the Student Referral template to the wellbeing team.
  5. The case appears on the Wellbeing Dashboard for counsellors to pick up.
  6. Keep status as Referred until wellbeing staff close or resolve the case with you.

12. Intervention lifecycle — visual summary

Alert appears on Dashboard ↓ Staff opens student profile ↓ Add Intervention (Initiated / In Progress) ↓ ┌──────────────────────────────────────┐ │ Ongoing follow-up contacts │ │ (more interventions or edits) │ └──────────────────────────────────────┘ ↓ ├──→ Resolved (student improved) ├──→ Referred (Wellbeing takes over) └──→ No Action Required (rare — document why)

13. Good practice checklist

  • ☐ Respond to red alerts as soon as possible — same week if you can.
  • ☐ Always add remarks — future staff (and you) need context.
  • ☐ Do not mark Resolved until the student’s alert has actually cleared or the issue is genuinely fixed.
  • ☐ Use Referred when wellbeing support is needed — do not try to be the counsellor yourself.
  • ☐ Check the dashboard weekly for Not Started cases in your scope.
  • ☐ Keep outreach professional and documented (email through the system when possible).

14. Quick reference — status meanings

StatusUse when…
Not StartedSystem only — student alerted, you have not acted yet.
InitiatedFirst contact made.
In ProgressOngoing support / multiple contacts.
ReferredHanded to Wellbeing Centre.
ResolvedStudent back on track; alert cleared or issue fixed.
No Action RequiredAlert reviewed; no action needed (explain in remarks).

15. Getting help

  • Cannot find a student? Check you are in the right faculty/department scope, or ask your HoD.
  • Form will not save? Make sure Date, Type, and Mode (if required) are filled in.
  • Wrong alert? Attendance may update when teachers post marks — wait for the next daily update.
  • Technical problems? Contact your faculty IT coordinator or system administrator.
